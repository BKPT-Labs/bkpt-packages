BKPT candidate architecture policy, version3; independent review required before publication. It extends version2, which stays in force for every profile it names.
Scope: STM32C542RCT6/LQFP64, NUCLEO-C542RC nominal NUC542RC$KR1 with PCB MB2213 B02, HAL2 pack2.1.0 and the captured c5-hal2-minimal foundation only. Board population, solder-bridge state, silicon revision and electrical setup remain unverified.
Inventory: pins, signal routes and interrupts transcribed from the pinout descriptor, device header and startup vector table record declared presence and routing. A route is not a capability, an electrical permission, a statement about board wiring or proof that two signals can be used together.
Debug access: the pads on board nets DBG.SWDIO and DBG.SWCLK carry the debug connection. They are occupied by the base and no capability may adopt them.
Clock input: the captured clock configuration runs from the external oscillator, so the pads on board nets OSC_IN and OSC_OUT are occupied by the base and no capability may adopt them.
General allocation: a configurable pad is eligible for general allocation only when its board net joins the device pad to connectors and solder bridges alone and no explicit exclusion names it. A pad whose net also carries another component, a debug or trace signal, an oscillator or a USB line stays outside general allocation until that circuit is reviewed. Eligibility is a conservative BKPT allocation rule, not an electrical qualification; solder-bridge state decides whether a connector actually reaches the pad.
Boot strap: the pad on board net PH2_BOOT0 selects the boot mode at reset and stays outside general allocation.
Console: the pads on board nets PA2 and PA3 are the routes the reviewed UART profiles use for the board console. They stay outside general allocation, and only a UART profile may take them.
GPIO output domain: any pad eligible for general allocation may be configured as a push-pull output through the reviewed GPIO output card. The application initializes the selected pad. Level, load, polarity and timing remain application obligations.
GPIO interrupt domain: any pad eligible for general allocation may be configured as an input with a CPU interrupt through the reviewed GPIO interrupt component. External line n serves pad index n of every port, so one line is one exclusive resource regardless of port. Interrupt priority5 is an authored integration choice, not a silicon limit.
SPI pin domain: the SCK, MISO and MOSI signals of a covered SPI instance may use any descriptor route to a pad eligible for general allocation; the alternate function comes from the selected route. The rates, mode and DMA rules of version2 are unchanged. Signal integrity at the selected rate on a given pad and connector is not verified.
Signal functions: a managed profile names a signal by the role it plays, so that one profile can serve several instances. The assignment is authored and explicit, and no tool derives it from a signal name. SPI1_SCK and SPI2_SCK are spi_sck. SPI1_MISO and SPI2_MISO are spi_miso. SPI1_MOSI and SPI2_MOSI are spi_mosi. USART1_TX, USART2_TX, UART4_TX and UART5_TX are uart_tx. USART1_RX, USART2_RX, UART4_RX and UART5_RX are uart_rx. A function states which role of the profile a signal plays and nothing about timing or electrical behaviour.
SPI instances: the managed SPI transfer profile covers SPI1 and SPI2. The device description derives the SPI2 register layout from SPI1, and under the captured clock configuration their kernel clocks PCLK2 and PCLK1 are both 144MHz, so the rate choices of version2 apply to either. SPI1 stays the first choice. I2S operation, the NSS and RDY signals, other kernel clock sources and any other SPI instance are not covered. The fixed SPI1 handle profile of version2 is unchanged.
UART instances: the managed UART transfer profile covers USART1, USART2, UART4 and UART5 in asynchronous 8N1 mode with a handle private to the generated unit. The device description derives the USART2, UART4 and UART5 register layouts from USART1. Each instance takes its kernel clock from its own bus clock, 144MHz under the captured clock configuration, and the SDK derives the divider from that clock at configuration time. USART2 on the console pads stays the first choice. LPUART1, synchronous clock output, hardware flow control and the fixed USART2 handle profiles of version2 are outside this rule.
UART pin domain: the transmit and receive signals of a covered UART instance may use any descriptor route to a console pad or to a pad eligible for general allocation; the alternate function comes from the selected route. Both pads are push-pull alternate outputs without pull, as in the captured USART2 configuration, so an unconnected receive pad floats. Signal integrity at the selected rate is not verified.
DMA request lines: a request line of a covered instance is recorded only when the reference manual request table, the SDK low-layer definition for each controller and the SDK enumerator agree on its selector. Every channel of both controllers can take any request, and one request may drive one active channel at a time, as version2 states for the lines it names.
User button: the pad on board net PC13 shares that net with push-button B1 and resistor R13, and the netlist places the other side of B1 on net 3V3 and the other side of R13 on net GND. Read as a button to the supply with a pull-down, a press reads high; that reading comes from net membership alone and is not checked against the schematic drawing or the populated board. The pad stays outside general allocation and may be allocated only as an interrupt input, because an output driven low would oppose the supply through a pressed button.
Timer channels: the timer channel allocation covers TIM1, TIM2, TIM8, TIM12 and TIM15 and the non-inverted channel signals listed here. It allocates a timer and one routed pad and generates no code. TIM1_CH1, TIM1_CH2, TIM1_CH3, TIM1_CH4, TIM2_CH1, TIM2_CH2, TIM2_CH3, TIM2_CH4, TIM8_CH1, TIM8_CH2, TIM8_CH3, TIM8_CH4, TIM12_CH1, TIM12_CH2, TIM15_CH1 and TIM15_CH2 are timer_channel. One counter serves every channel of a timer, so an allocation takes the whole timer and two allocations never share one. Period, duty, polarity, compare or capture use, complementary outputs, dead time and break inputs are application decisions, and no timing is claimed.
Timer channel symbols: the SDK channel symbol of a listed signal is the one the SDK describes as the timer channel of that number. TIM1_CH1, TIM2_CH1, TIM8_CH1, TIM12_CH1 and TIM15_CH1 are HAL_TIM_CHANNEL_1. TIM1_CH2, TIM2_CH2, TIM8_CH2, TIM12_CH2 and TIM15_CH2 are HAL_TIM_CHANNEL_2. TIM1_CH3, TIM2_CH3 and TIM8_CH3 are HAL_TIM_CHANNEL_3. TIM1_CH4, TIM2_CH4 and TIM8_CH4 are HAL_TIM_CHANNEL_4. The pairing does not state that every mode of a channel exists on every timer.
Analog inputs: the analog input allocation covers ADC1 and the external input signals listed here. It allocates one routed pad for analog use and generates no code. ADC1_IN0, ADC1_IN1, ADC1_IN2, ADC1_IN3, ADC1_IN4, ADC1_IN5, ADC1_IN6, ADC1_IN7, ADC1_IN8, ADC1_IN9, ADC1_IN10 and ADC1_IN11 are adc_input. The pads PC4, PC5, PB0 and PB1 reach inputs IN2, IN5, IN6 and IN7 only after the SBS pin remap, which this rule does not set, so they are outside it and every covered input has exactly one pad. The converter is one shared unit that up to ten allocations may name at once. Sequencing, sampling time, calibration, reference voltage, source impedance and accuracy are application decisions and are not claimed. Internal channels, injected conversions and differential inputs are outside this rule.
Analog channel symbols: the SDK describes each of these symbols as the converter channel to the pin signal of the same number. ADC1_IN0 is HAL_ADC_CHANNEL_0. ADC1_IN1 is HAL_ADC_CHANNEL_1. ADC1_IN2 is HAL_ADC_CHANNEL_2. ADC1_IN3 is HAL_ADC_CHANNEL_3. ADC1_IN4 is HAL_ADC_CHANNEL_4. ADC1_IN5 is HAL_ADC_CHANNEL_5. ADC1_IN6 is HAL_ADC_CHANNEL_6. ADC1_IN7 is HAL_ADC_CHANNEL_7. ADC1_IN8 is HAL_ADC_CHANNEL_8. ADC1_IN9 is HAL_ADC_CHANNEL_9. ADC1_IN10 is HAL_ADC_CHANNEL_10. ADC1_IN11 is HAL_ADC_CHANNEL_11.
All capability domains remain open/incomplete. Resource allocation, materialization, compilation and hardware observation are distinct; no complete errata/electrical/application qualification is claimed.
